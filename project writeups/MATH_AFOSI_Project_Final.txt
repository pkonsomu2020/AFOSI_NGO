AFOSI PROJECT TEMPLATE
1. Project Title
The M.A.T.H Project - Mazingira, Afya, Tumaini, na Haki yetu 
2.What This Project Is About
The M.A.T.H Project is a three-year initiative (2025-2028) implemented by AFOSI in 60 Alternative Provision of Basic Education and Training (APBET) schools in Kibera and Mukuru, two of Nairobi's largest informal settlements. The project supports the review, sensitization, and implementation of Kenya's Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) Policy for the Education Sector (2017), targeting school communities- owners, administrators, learners, and residents  who lack the knowledge, skills, and platforms to address climate change and environmental degradation.
• What it does: Delivers ESD education and sensitization to APBET school communities; advocates for the review and implementation of the ESD Policy (2017) with the Ministry of Education; and fosters green innovation and circular economy entrepreneurship among youth through incubation, competitions, and linkage to the Youth Climate Hub
• Who it serves: Children, youth, teachers, APBET school owners and administrators, community members, persons with disabilities, and government duty bearers in Kibera and Mukuru 
• Overall Goal: Children and young people realize their rights through increased knowledge and strengthened resilience within their communities, ensuring their well-being, protection, participation and equal access to a healthy and sustainable environment
3.Why It Matters
• Kenya's ESD Policy for the Education Sector (2017) mandates integration of climate change, poverty alleviation, human right, and environmental protection into the education system yet the Presidential Working Party on Education Reform identified that the concept of ESD is not fully understood, implementation strategies are absent, and ESD has not reached the  APBET 
• Children and youth in Kibera and Mukuru bear the heaviest burden of climate change - flood-driven waterborne disease, respiratory illness from indoor pollution, school disruptions, and rising gender-based violence - yet current policies do not adequately respect, protect, or fulfil their rights to a clean, safe, and healthy environment, and there are no platforms for youth to develop, showcase, and incubate climate innovations
4.What We Do
The project works through three integrated pillars - Education and Sensitization, Advocacy, and Innovation:
• Education and Sensitization: Trains APBET owners, school leaders, teachers,and learners in 60 targeted schools on ESD, using animation, gamification, and STEM4Sustainable Development tools (creative coding, AI-enabled early warning systems) to spark climate curiosity among children and embed sustainability across school curricula and community practices
• Advocacy: Support the Ministry of Education to review the ESD Policy for the Education Sector (2017) so that it aligns with Presidential Working Party on Education Reform (PWPER) recommendations and explicitly targets APBET schools; partners with the First Lady's Mazingira Awards (FLAMA) to raise environmentally responsible citizens; and amplifies youth voices in local and national climate policy processes through trained advocacy clubs and structured dialogue forums with duty bearers
• Innovation: Organize platforms for APBET schools to showcase ESD ideas and innovations; selects winning projects and incubates them for at least 12 months, adding value beyond the FLAMA showcasing stage; links youth innovators to the Youth Climate Hub (co-developed with STEM Impact Centre Kenya and UNICEF Kenya) for long-term scale and green entrepreneurship development
• Community Engagement: Mobilize children and young people to lead quarterly environmental clean-ups, tree planting, kitchen gardens, and eco-fairs; establishes 60 functional Climate Change Action Advocacy clubs; and trains grassroots journalists on digital storytelling to document and share climate action from the ground up
5. Key Solutions and Programs
The project is structured around five outputs that operationalize the three pillars of Education, Advocacy, and Innovation:
Output 1.1 - ESD Education and Capacity Building (60 APBET Schools)
• Maps and identifies 60 APBET project schools, partners, and stakeholders. Develops 3 ESD training curricula and facilitator handbooks tailored for APBET owners, teachers, and learners. Trains 30 Trainers of Trainers (ToTs) to cascade ESD training across the 60 schools. Conducts a 5-day leadership training and workshop series for 240 participants - 60 APBET owners, 60 school management team members, and 120 teachers - on integrating ESD into APBET programs and building skills to manage sustainable education initiatives
• Integrates STEM4Sustainable Development annually into 60 APBET schools using creative coding for climate and gamified content, sparking children's curiosity and problem-solving. Holds quarterly radio talk shows to disseminate ESD messages. Launches an ESD Integration Challenge rewarding schools with innovative climate education practices, tracked through lesson plans and classroom observations. Develops community-based kitchen gardens in all 60 schools to promote sustainable food systems. Trains 20 journalists on ESD and child protection reporting. Trains 190 APBET owners, teachers, and staff annually through virtual workshops on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and the climate change nexus
Output 1.2 - Environmental Campaigns and Community Stewardship
• Organises quarterly environmental clean-up activities involving students, teachers, and 600 community members per cycle to promote environmental stewardship in Kibera and Mukuru. Hosts annual eco-fairs where students and community members showcase sustainable practices, green technologies, and local environmental projects. Holds annual thematic events where students report and advocate for environmental issues in their communities
• Conducts annual ESD essay competitions among APBET students, awarding the 10 best writers and amplifying at least 3 climate action stories through social media. Trains 20 grassroots journalists annually on digital storytelling to document and share climate action initiatives through digital and social media platforms, giving communities a voice in national conversations
Output 1.3 - Climate Change Clubs in 60 APBET Institutions
• Establishes or strengthens 60 sustainability clubs in all targeted APBET schools, where students engage in tree planting, energy conservation projects, and awareness campaigns, with shared resources, case studies, and collaborative best practices documented across schools. Conducts a 3-day training for 20 mentors covering ESD facilitation, club management, and child protection standards
• Organises 2 inter-school ESD innovation competitions for 60 students to showcase their innovations and climate solutions. Facilitates 20 top students to participate in national ESD showcase platforms such as the Kenya Innovation Week, connecting school-level ideas to wider audiences and opportunities
Output 2.1 - ESD Policy Review and Pilot Implementation
• Engages a consultant to conduct a comparative study of global best practices for integrating ESD into informal education systems, synthesizing findings into a policy brief presented to policymakers at a national workshop. Actively supports the Ministry of Education to review Kenya's ESD Policy for the Education Sector (2017) to align it with PWPER recommendations and to ensure APBET schools are explicitly included in its scope and implementation. Holds quarterly ESD Technical Working Group meetings producing documented minutes and policy revision recommendations submitted to government authorities
• Documents and disseminates climate change and ESD stories through child-led audio/visual storytelling platforms, making children's perspectives visible in policy spaces. Organises annual advocacy media campaigns targeting county education officers, parliamentary committees, and Ministry of Education representatives - producing policy briefs and securing documented government commitments toward ESD policy implementation in APBET schools
Output 2.2 - Youth and Community-Led Advocacy Platforms
• Organises annual workshops training 40 young people in leadership, public speaking, and advocacy skills, empowering them to effectively participate in climate and ESD policy discussions. Identifies and connects at least 50 young people annually to local, national, and international policy platforms. Hosts at least 2 virtual/physical town hall and community dialogue meetings at national and county level where young people interact directly with policymakers, ask questions, and share their views on ESD and climate rights
• Arranges visits to successful green businesses and recycling facilities to give youth practical insights into sustainable livelihoods. Partners with the First Lady's Mazingira Awards (FLAMA) to support school-level ESD advocacy at national level. Holds quarterly joint proposal development and fundraising events. Conducts joint case documentation studies on pressing issues including GBV prevention, care economy, and climate adaptation. Organises 3 annual peer learning and reflection sessions across implementing partners to share lessons, document adaptive practices, and foster a culture of accountability and continuous improvement
Output 2.3 - Green Innovation and Circular Economy Entrepreneurship
• Launches annual innovation competitions engaging at least 100 youth to propose solutions to environmental and climate challenges in their communities. Organises intensive 5-day bootcamps annually for 100 youth, equipping them with business planning, financing, and sustainable innovation skills. Supports the FLAMA initiative by awarding at least 10 ESD winners annually with recognition, mentorship, and media visibility - going beyond the showcasing stage by incubating 20 young innovators (including FLAMA winners) with access to mentorship, funding pathways, and training for at least 12 months, and linking them to the Youth Climate Hub co-established with STEM Impact Centre Kenya and UNICEF Kenya
• Celebrates 5 annual children and climate justice action days (Day of the African Child, Africa Climate Summit, International Day Against Climate Change, International Day of the Girl Child, World Clean-Up Day) to amplify children's voices in climate spaces. Conducts quarterly staff capacity building on gender-responsive programming (gender, governance, safeguarding) to strengthen organizational and programmatic competencies. Acquires and implements an Integrated Enterprise Resource Planning system integrating Human Resources, programme management, and Monitoring and Evaluation. Conducts quarterly board supervision visits to all project implementation sites to enhance accountability
6.Who It Serves
Primary beneficiaries of the M.A.T.H Project:
• Children and young people  with deliberate inclusion of girls, children with disabilities, and other underrepresented groups
• APBET school owners, teachers, school management teams, and children across the 60 targeted schools - building their capacity on ESD  to deliver inclusive, rights-based, and climate-responsive education
• Community members (including 200 mobilised per quarterly clean-up cycle), persons with disabilities, grassroots journalists, and duty bearers - the Ministry of Education, Nairobi County Government, county education officers, and parliamentary committees - engaged as partners in ESD policy review and implementation
7. Impact
By 2028, the M.A.T.H Project expects to achieve the following outcomes, contributing to the overall goal that children and young people realise their rights through increased knowledge and strengthened resilience:
• At least 75% of targeted children, youth, teachers, community members, and duty bearers in 60 APBET schools demonstrate improved knowledge of climate change, biodiversity, and environmental sustainability, and are actively engaged as environmental stewardship actors and advocates - with at least 80% of climate education sessions integrating gender-transformative content and achieving =50% female participation
• A responsive and enabling policy environment upholds children's and young people's rights to a clean, safe, and healthy environment: Kenya's ESD Policy (2017) is reviewed and piloted for implementation in targeted APBET schools; at least 60% of trained children and youth participate in at least one environmental/climate advocacy platform annually; and at least 2 climate-related policy processes are influenced by documented contributions from children, youth, CSOs, and communities
• Children, young people, and communities are better prepared to manage environmental impacts while addressing poverty and structural inequalities: at least 20 youth innovators are incubated and linked to the Youth Climate Hub; new climate-smart enterprises and livelihood initiatives are started by youth; and =60% of targeted households demonstrate adoption of at least one new preparedness, adaptation, or climate-smart livelihood practice as a result of project support
